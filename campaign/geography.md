## Calendar

The Forgotten Realms uses **Dalereckoning (DR)**. Year 1 DR marks the raising of the Standing Stone and the pact between the elves of Cormanthor and the first human settlers of the Dalelands — roughly 1,500 years before the sourcebook's present. Years are colloquially known by names (e.g., 1479 DR = *Year of the Ageless One*). The standard calendar is the **Calendar of Harptos**: 12 months of 30 days, five special intercalary days marking the seasons, and a leap day called **Shieldmeet** every four years (following Midsummer night). **(CG)**

## The Dalelands — Overview

The Dalelands comprises several forested counties — called **dales** — scattered around the perimeter of the **Cormanthor Forest**. The dales share common traditions, cultural practices, and religious allegiances, but are **not a unified kingdom**. Broad forest vales separated by rolling farmlands are linked by narrow trade roads through lush woods. Their independent spirit and age-old alliance with the fey of Cormanthor are well known across Faerûn. **(CG)**

The region generally lies in or near the valley carved by the **River Ashaba** and its tributaries. **(PG)**

**Dalesfolk** are predominantly human, fiercely independent, suspicious of expansionistic empires, and reject rule by sword or spell. They view their lands as a trust to be held for future generations. Most live in harmony with elves; many half-elves (especially in Deepingdale) are products of those unions. A typical Dalesperson wants only to farm and hunt in peace — but keeps a sword and bow close and volunteers for the local militia at the first sign of trouble. **(PG)**

**Regional benefit:** Dalesfolk can reroll any Nature check (must keep the second result) and gain a +1 bonus on initiative checks. **(PG)**

## The Free Dales

### Archendale
**Militaristic Dale | Population ~90,000 | Capital: Archenbridge (pop. 8,000)**

Known as the unfriendliest and most aggressive of the dales — and also the toughest and best defended. The vigilance of both citizens and soldiers lets Archenfolk pursue their love of trade. The capital, **Archenbridge**, is the Dalelands' most fortified city: cramped, bustling, with cobbled streets and buildings rising three or more floors, all featuring barred windows and hidden inner holds equipped with arrow slits. Though it nearly fell to Sembian expansionism, Archenbridge maintains trade with its southern neighbor — trade that fuels the city's thriving markets and merchant houses. **(CG)**

- **Governance:** Following the unmasking and removal of the secretive autocrats known as "the Swords" (found to be in Sembia's pocket, 1440 DR), Archendale now tempers its greed for trade with a significant concern for defense. **(CG)**
- **Relations:** Strained with Myth Drannor; friendly to Cormyr; cautious with Sembia. **(CG)**
- **Notable faction:** The **Hidden Hand of Fate** — a small cult of **Hoar** (the Doombringer) has dwelt in a temple fortress deep in the **Arch Wood** for centuries. They sponsor troubadours, bounty hunters, and assassins throughout the Heartlands, coordinating a loose network of vigilantes dealing rough justice. Archenfolk's reputation for short tempers, grudges, and love of intrigue feeds a quiet following of Hoar. **(CG)**

---

### Battledale
**Tranquil Dale | Population ~30,000**

Despite its name, Battledale today is a guarded but peaceful land — the name comes from centuries of bloody warfare between greater powers long ago. Large in area, it is one of the least populous and most dispersed of the dales. Visitors see only open, rolling farmlands along **Rauthauvyr's Road** (called "the Belt" by Dalesfolk). In summer, marvelous camping country — but beware brigands and the occasional **Eldreth Veluuthra** hunting party from the **Tangled Trees**. **(CG)**

- **Essembra** — Deep in the thick woods lies the ghost town of **Essembra**: a long lane of abandoned, fire-ruined cottages rotting on both sides of Rauthauvyr's Road. About sixty years ago, it was the site of a fierce battle between Myth Drannor's forces and Netherese-backed Sembia. Fell magic prompted the locals to abandon their homes. **(CG/PG)**
- **Ambrose (pop. ~3,000)** — A small town of hearty folk devoted to **Tempus**, grown up around the much-expanded **Abbey of the Sword**. The abbey was established by Priest Eldan Ambrose where the god Tempus was said to have arrived in Faerûn during the Time of Troubles (1358 DR). Ambrose himself was slain in a Vhaeraunian drow attack; the defenders repelled the drow but couldn't resurrect him. **(CG)**
- **The Zhent Headless Horseman** — A lich wearing the colors of the overthrown Zhentarim that gallops forest crossings and night mists. Bodies of would-be heroes turn up headless when the mist clears. A haunting reminder of the Black Network's former power in the northern dales. **(CG)**

### Daggerdale
**Nightmarish Dale | Population ~15,000 | Capital: Dagger Falls (pop. ~2,000)**

Most of Daggerdale is rolling wooded hills broken by labyrinthine rocky valleys — good grazing country for livestock, but large ranches are confined to the region around **Dagger Falls** (the name applies both to a steep waterfall and to the nearby frontier town). **(CG)**

- **Desertsmouth Mountains** tower over the hills to the west. The ruins of **Tethyamar**, a great dwarven kingdom that fell to orcs, ogres, evil wizards, and fiends several hundred years ago, lie within the peaks. Some of those threats still lurk there. Adventuring companies frequently seek Tethyamar ruins. **(CG)**
- **Threats:** Daggerdale has been increasingly troubled by savage humanoids raiding from the Desertsmouth Mountains and by a resurgence of lycanthropes in the crags of the **Dagger Hills**. The frightened villagers of Dagger Falls have become isolated and cut off from the other dales. **(CG)**
- **History:** A century ago, Daggerdale was ruled by Zhentarim agents — a distant memory today, though tensions linger. **(CG)**

### Deepingdale
**Welcoming Dale | Regional Population ~50,000 | Capital: Highmoon (pop. ~8,000)**

Situated along the contested route between Cormyr and Sembia. Deepingdale comprises the lands around the **River Glaemril** and the **River Wineflow**, both swift and strong with abundant salmon runs. More than any other dale, Deepingdale is woven into and around Cormanthor. Half the population is of elven or half-elven ancestry; the Deepingfolk live in the shadow of the trees and in the lighter growth at the forest's outskirts. **(CG)**

- **Highmoon** continues to prosper, despite diminished trade through the Eldreth Veluuthra-controlled **Hullack Forest** to the west. **(CG)**
- **Temple of Lathander, Highmoon** *(DM ADDITION, Session 005)* — In S5, Lady Ulphor handed **Naan** a private letter of introduction and invitation to study with the clergy of **Lathander** (the Morninglord) here, or to use the temple's resources. The DM framed Highmoon to the players as "the nearby regional capital." Naan read it but did not disclose it to the party; the invitation is a **live, undisclosed hook** competing with the Thorn Lord's audience. *(The Lathander temple is a DM addition; Highmoon and Deepingdale are canonical [CG].)*

### Harrowdale
**Oldest Dale | Population ~44,000 | Capital: New Velar (pop. ~9,000)**

Harrowdale borders Cormanthor to the west, the **Dragon Reach** to the east, and the **Cold Field** to the south. The countryside is mostly farmlands, turning to orchards closer to the sea and to the **Velarswood** at its center. **(CG)**

- **New Velar** — Formerly the small seaside village of Harrowdale Town, now a rich mercantile port and the largest free settlement in the Dalelands (since Scardale's fall to Sembia). The city is connected to Myth Drannor by the **Harrow Ride** (formerly the Halfaxe Trail, now a well-guarded paved road through the old Elven Court). Goods from across the Sea of Fallen Stars flow through New Velar on their way to and from Myth Drannor, making the city enormously wealthy. **(CG)**
- **Governance:** Ruled by the **Council of Seven Burghers** — the city's wealthiest merchants. A Burgher holds the position for life; upon a death, the wealthiest non-Burgher fills the seat. **(CG)**
- **Myth Drannor alliance:** The Seven Burghers formally allied with Myth Drannor in 1428 DR and renamed the city. Myth Drannor maintains an embassy here and built shipyards nearby. **(CG)**
- **The Fall of Stars** — A famous adventurers' club belonging to the **Stellar Fellowship**, housed in a row of quietly luxurious old townhouses. Behind the facade, a huge feasting hall occupies the former stable yard. Injured members receive potions, antidotes, and medicines freely. **(CG)**

### High Dale
**Cormyrian Dale | Population ~15,000**

High Dale is tucked just below the main section of the Dalelands and found it necessary to ally with **Cormyr** to stave off Sembian advances (1422 DR). **Purple Dragon Knights** of Cormyr are stationed here, making it effectively a Cormyrian protectorate. The dale is on friendly terms with Cormyr though some Dalesfolk resent the arrangement. **(CG)**

---

### Mistledale
**Largest Free Dale | Capital: Ashabenford**

Ashabenford, the capital, sprawls across the banks of the River Ashaba and serves as the seat of the **Dales Council** — the closest thing the Dalelands has to a unified government. The Council has consolidated real authority since Sembia began conquering dales. The city's relative security and proximity to Myth Drannor have made it wealthy. **(CG/PG)**

- **The Dales Compact** — The age-old pact between the dales and Myth Drannor was reaffirmed in 1425 DR. **(CG)**
- **The Standing Stone** — The great symbol of the original pact between Cormanthor's elves and the first Dalesfolk (circa year 1 DR). It was destroyed by drow roughly fifty years before the sourcebook present, then restored in 1425 DR. The stone stands as a powerful emblem of Dalelands identity and independence. **(CG)**
- **Threats:** Drow plague Mistledale (and Battledale and Shadowdale); the Dales Council keeps a wary eye on Cormyrian expansion through **Tilver's Gap** to the southwest. **(CG)**

---

### Shadowdale
**Quiet Dale | Capital: the village of Shadowdale**

Shadowdale remains peaceful and secure despite the loss of many of its legendary protectors (Storm Silverhand has departed). **Elminster of Shadowdale** still resides here, though his powers have diminished since Mystra's fall. The border with the **Feywild** is weak along the **River Ashaba** in Shadowdale; a burgeoning population of fey and sylvan creatures has appeared throughout the land. **(CG)**

- **Tone:** The gentlest of the dales — but the thin Feywild border makes for unpredictable magical encounters. Elminster's presence draws scholars, sages, and power-seekers from across Faerûn.
- **Threats:** Drow incursions (shared with Battledale and Mistledale); Feywild bleed-through; opportunists drawn by Elminster's reputation. **(CG)**

## The Occupied Dales

### Featherdale
**Sembian Puppet | Population ~18,000**

Featherdale isn't technically a dale (it's not a valley). Its rich farming lands fall within the sweep of the River Ashaba as it flows east toward the Sea of Fallen Stars — a patchwork of gently rolling farms with fieldstone walls and evergreen levees against flooding. It's the pastoral farming country that many Sembians imagine the entire Dalelands to be. Sembian investors gradually bought up so much land that administrators eventually moved in; the **Shadovar of Netheril** "accidentally" conquered it in 1418 DR when their Sembian proxies took over. The puppet state pays little attention to this stretch of road. Unlike other Dalesfolk, Featherdarrans have little to do with Myth Drannor — a separation made deeper by the influx of Sembian merchants who collect taxes and enforce trade policies through well-paid mercenaries. **(CG)**

---

### Scardale
**Sembian-Occupied | Capital: Scardale Town (formerly Chandlerscross)**

Scardale fell to Sembia through a long campaign of betrayals, merchant deals, and slow consolidations of power, ending when a Sembian mercenary army appeared in the capital, slaughtered many Dalelanders, and drove out the rest. Scardale Town, at the mouth of the River Ashaba, is now a den of intrigue: Sembian merchant lords, Thayan red-robed wizards, and Moonsea warlords all jostle for position here. **(CG/PG)**

---

### Tasseldale
**Sembian-Occupied**

When Sembia could not subvert Tasseldale as easily as Featherdale, mercenaries financed out of **Yhaunn** overran the area in 1420 DR. Requests for aid from Archendale were rebuffed. **(CG)**

## Surrounding Regions

### Cormanthor Forest
The vast and ancient elven forest at the heart of the Dalelands — the dales are arranged around its perimeter. Dense, old-growth woodland with deep wilderness between the settled trade roads. Home to the elven city of Myth Drannor and a sanctuary for fey and sylvan creatures. Also harbors drow portals, Eldreth Veluuthra hunting parties, and the Tangled Trees. **(CG)**

### Myth Drannor
The restored elven capital within Cormanthor, protected by an ancient **mythal** (a powerful magical ward). The coronal rules from here. Myth Drannor is the Dalelands' most important ally against Sembia and Netheril, maintains an embassy in New Velar, and serves as the Dales Compact's elven signatory. The mythal also blocks portals and prevents planar travel within its bounds. **(CG)**

### Cormyr
The Forest Kingdom to the southwest. A monarchy ruled by House Obarskyr with a warm alliance with Myth Drannor and the Dalelands. Cormyr has stationed **Purple Dragon Knights** in High Dale and provides a military backstop against Netherese and Sembian expansion. The Dalelands' relationship with Cormyr is generally positive, though Mistledale watches Cormyrian expansion through Tilver's Gap nervously. **(CG)**

### Sembia
A merchant kingdom to the south, now a vassal state of the **Empire of Netheril**. Sembia has swallowed several dales through a combination of economic subversion, mercenary force, and outright conquest. An ongoing threat to all free dales. **(CG)**

### The Empire of Netheril
Ancient empire restored from beneath the **Anauroch Desert**, now ruled by the Twelve Princes of Shade from their floating capital, **Shade Enclave**. Netheril manipulates Sembia as a proxy and is the looming existential threat on the northern horizon — beyond the **Desertsmouth Mountains**. **(CG)**

### The Moonsea Region
To the north of the Dalelands. An unsettled and dangerous region that continually troubles the free dales. **(CG)**

## DM Additions

### Old Skull — Northern Flank Cleft Chain *(DM ADDITION)*

**Old Skull** is the bald granite hill rising at the north edge of Shadowdale village, named for its pale, weatherworn dome. The hill is a known feature of Shadowdale; the **cleft chain** along its northern and northwestern flanks is a campaign-specific fixture established in sessions 001–003.

A series of three narrow vertical fissures (locally called *clefts*) pierce the hill's flanks, each roughly a 20–40-foot drop into a small antechamber at the surface, then a steeper crawl down into the upper Underdark passages beneath Old Skull. The clefts are old, weather-worked, and natural in origin — but the **Vhaeraunian drow surface cell** has been using them as staging mouths for surface operations. The clefts run roughly west-to-east along the hill's northern shoulder, separated by a few hundred yards of pine-wooded slope each.

| Site | Location | Status (post-S4) | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Site A** | Western cleft, lower slope, near the village wood-line | **Closed (S1)** — collapsed and warded by the party | First Vhaeraunian staging mouth the party encountered; **Zeldrazz T'orrl** was the field officer here |
| **Site B / The Hawthorn Chimney** | Center cleft, mid-slope, beneath an old hawthorn windbreak | **Closed (S2, by Lolthites)** — vertical shaft collapsed from below | Lolthite raiders purged this site before the party arrived; party found a cold camp and the after-action signs of a crawl-tunnel infiltration |
| **Site C / The Granite Shoulder** | Eastern cleft, upper northern flank, on a broad granite shoulder above the Living Wood edge | **Cleared (S3)** — Vhaeraunian shrine-camp neutralized; portal never opened | Priest **Khelziir Aun'velve** and bodyguard **Zeldrazz T'orrl** both killed; portal-anchor pendant recovered (now in Lady Ulphor's vault). Spider Pen bypassed; Lolthite Crash never triggered |

**Surrounding terrain:**
- **The Living Wood** — the band of fey-touched pine and hawthorn forest skirting Old Skull's northern flank. The fey of the granite shoulder withdrew during the drow incursion; with Site C cleared (S3), **Quillip Burr-and-Whistle** now ranges freely again (she turned up in the village for the S4 Greengrass Festival). Whether the rest of the wood's fey return is open
- **The Hawthorn Chimney** — surface name for the cold camp at Site B, marked by a distinctive twin-trunked hawthorn at the cleft mouth
- **The Granite Shoulder** — broad rock terrace above the Living Wood; Site C's surface antechamber opens onto it

**Travel:** Site A → Site B is roughly 2 hours overland on the cleft-line trail; Site B → Site C another 3 hours. The whole chain is within a half-day's hike from Shadowdale village.

**Why it matters:** The Vhaeraunian cell treats the cleft chain as a single piece of infrastructure — surface anchors for a planned portal network into Shadowdale. The Lolthites under Old Skull are walking the same line, closing each cleft from below. Whichever faction wins the cleft chain wins the surface foothold under Shadowdale.

> [!NOTE]
> **Source notes**
> Old Skull itself is canonical Shadowdale geography *[CG]*. The northern cleft chain, Sites A/B/C, the Hawthorn Chimney name, the Granite Shoulder, and the Living Wood holdout are DM additions established in play across sessions 001–003.

### Shadowdale Village — Greengrass Festival Grounds *(DM ADDITION)*

The **Greengrass Festival** (the Faerûnian spring holiday marking the end of winter) is celebrated annually in Shadowdale, established in play in Session 004. The village dresses its fences with flowers, stacks a head-high **bonfire on the river stones**, and ties ribbons through the center of town. Festival venues, all within the village footprint:

- **Town center / festival stage** — Lady Ulphor opens the festival and presents flower crowns here; site of the **Bardic Showcase** and the central proceedings.
- **Wood's edge** — site of **The Greening** (herbalism / animal-handling event, run by Sela Wyndmere) and the traditional **sapling-planting blessing** (a new tree is planted each Greengrass).
- **Near the Old Skull Inn** — Brynn Ashford runs a bookstall; archery and other contests are set up nearby along the riverbank.
- **The River Ashaba** — during the festival, **barges cross the Ashaba eastward toward Nettledale**; mounted/boat archery targets float on the river.

> [!NOTE]
> **Source notes**
> Greengrass is a canonical Faerûnian holiday *[CG/PG]*. The specific Shadowdale festival layout, events, and the Nettledale barge crossings are DM additions established in Session 004.

### The Living Wood — The Recall and the Center of the Blight *(DM ADDITION)*

The **Living Wood** north-east of Shadowdale, beyond the Granite Shoulder, took center stage in Session 005. Walking in from the village side, the first half-mile is ordinary spring — birdsong, blackthorn in bloom — and then the color bleaches out: blossoms go from white to gray, birdsong fades, "the forest is packing up and leaving." From the canopy (Dirk's climb, S5), the wood is **green for a few miles back toward town and gray in every other direction** — a widening **circle of blight centered on the wood's center,** where the **court of the Thorn Lord** (see [factions](factions.md) §8) lies.

The blight is a **collapsing Feywild seam:** the thin border that keeps this wood half-enchanted is **contracting inward** toward the center, recalling the fey and the wood's ambient enchantment "like water down a drain" (Yinu, Arcana 30, S5). The fey are **not fleeing the drow — they are being recalled.** **Sela Wyndmere's ribbon-knots,** tied at intervals on the trees, were the **stitching holding the seam open;** with her pact broken (S5), the seam retracts faster.

**The court at the center** is where the unnamed wood-lord (the **Thorn Lord / Living Lord**) will receive the marked party — the destination of the two-day summons delivered at the bonfire. Prep imagined the audience ground as a hawthorn-arch clearing (two gnarled hawthorns grown into a living arch, seven ribboned standing stones, the seam pulled tight into a shining doorway), guarded by **Maelorn the Thorn Ward** (see [roster](roster.md)) — but **the party did not reach it in S5** (see status note).

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **DM:** Status after Session 005 (played)
> The party **did not reach the court.** The session **cut on the approach** to the wood's center. There was **no gate-test, no combat, and no audience** — those carry forward to **Session 006.** The prepped "frightened-wood beasts" (fey-touched stag and wolves) **did not appear;** instead the party calmed **two stranded goats** in the skill challenge. The audience clock is **two days** (not a full moon). **(DM ADDITION, Session 005)**

> [!NOTE]
> **Source notes**
> The Living Wood and its thin Feywild border are canonical Shadowdale geography *[CG]*. The contracting seam / recall, the circle of blight, the ribbon-stitching, and the Thorn Lord's court are DM additions established in Session 005.
