## Adventure Summary

This is the audience Session 005 walked toward and cut before — delivered at last, in **two acts and roughly two hours.** The party is at the greying center of the Living Wood with **Sela Wyndmere** between them, the seam contracting to a single bright point ahead. **Act One is the gate:** **Maelorn the Thorn Ward** holds the seam-door and runs the trial the Court owes every guest — a measuring, not a killing, that **collapses into ceremony if Nalith still carries Quillip's hawthorn berry,** and that forces a choice on the unmarked sixth, **Dirk.** **Act Two is the table:** through the door, the party is received by the lord himself — **Oberon, the Green Lord,** named at last — gracious, ancient, and generous, laying a feast and offering the marked an eternal place at his court. The grace **is** the harvest. The same slow pull that emptied the wood is now closing on the party, and the "taken children" of Shadowdale are already here, woven smiling into the hall.

The session lands on **Sela's redemption.** She tied twelve years of stitching that let Oberon reel the wood in; she is the only one who can **cut it** — and the fey healing he loaned her is the thread she has to spend to do it. She gives her life to sever the seam from inside, breaking the lord's hold on the party. But the binding collapses the door, and instead of the road home the backlash **hurls the party through the failing seam into the raw Feywild.** Hard cut. Sela is gone; the company is alive, free, cheated Oberon of his catch — and lost in his world.

> [!WARNING]
> **Flag:** DM — Scope is deliberately two acts
> This session is **the gate and the table — nothing past the threshold.** Do **not** play out the Feywild on the far side; the tumble through is the closing image, and where they land is **Session 007's** problem. If the table is flying, end early rather than starting an act you can't finish. The point of the night is Oberon's reveal and **Sela's death,** not a third location.

# PART ONE — The Gate

*~50 minutes. The last mile, the gatekeeper's trial, and the door.*

---

## Scene 1 — The Last Mile

### Setup

Pick up exactly where Session 005 cut: the party deep in the Living Wood, the color drained out of everything, **Sela Wyndmere** walking point the way she'd walk a sick-room. Ahead the grey thins into a hard line, and past it — the only living color left in the forest — two great hawthorns lean together over a doorway made of standing light.

> [!NOTE]
> **Read-aloud:** Read Aloud
> You have been walking the wrong way down the road out of town for an hour, and the wood has been dying around you the whole way — color first, then birdsong, then warmth. Now the grey ends like a held breath. Past the last dead trees the ground rises into a ring of moss-furred standing stones, and at its center two ancient hawthorns have grown together overhead, white with the only blossom left in the world. Beneath them the air **stands up like water on its end** — a doorway of green-gold light, pulled tight and bright and *breathing.* Sela stops at the treeline and does not take another step. *"This is as far as I have ever been let,"* she says, very quietly. *"He has never let me see the door."*

*(Player handout: **The Hawthorn Gate** — see `the-green-lords-court-3-player-handouts.md`.)*

### Three Things Before the Gate *(keep it to ~10 minutes)*

The party has eleven loose threads in their packs; do **not** relitigate them here. Land at most these three quick reorientation beats and move to the gate:

- **The seam is closing on a clock.** Any **Arcana DC 13** (Yinu +11) confirms what Yinu read last session: the seam is still contracting, faster now, drawing toward the bright point under the arch. *Whatever is going to happen happens on the far side, and the door will not stand open forever.*
- **Sela has come to the end of her road — and means to go further.** She was only ever the courier; she has never crossed. Now she intends to. If asked why: *"Because he should have to say it to my face. And because I tied the knots that are strangling this wood. If there's a thread to cut, I won't have you cutting it blind."* (Foreshadows her arc; do not explain it.)
- **Quillip is not here yet.** The pixie did not come to the bonfire and has not appeared on the walk. If the party calls for her, a single hawthorn leaf spirals down with a knot tied in its stem — *she is near the gate, and she is afraid to come closer.* (She turns up at the gate, Scene 2.)

> [!WARNING]
> **Flag:** DM — Don't re-run Session 5
> Yinu's Harper book, Naan's Lathander letter, the Vhaeraunian clock — all still live, all **off the table tonight.** If a player reaches for one, one sentence of acknowledgment and back to the door. The compulsion is pulling them forward; let the scene pull too.

### What This Scene Locks In

- The party is **at the gate** with Sela, the seam visibly contracting on a clock.
- Sela has declared she is **crossing** — the Thornward's *"bring her"* (S5 prep) is hereby honored by her own choice.
- The door is the only way on, and the gatekeeper is about to step out of it.

## Scene 2 — The Hawthorn Gate

*~35 minutes. The trial the Court owes every guest. Combat, ceremony, or both.*

### Setup

As the party approaches the arch, the light parts and the gatekeeper steps out of it. This is the gate-trial **prepped for Session 005 and never reached** — run it now. It is a **measuring, not a killing:** Maelorn pulls every blow, yields the instant the party shows him something worth naming, and **collapses the whole thing into one ceremonial round if Nalith spends Quillip's hawthorn berry.**

> [!NOTE]
> **Read-aloud:** Read Aloud — The Thorn Ward
> The doorway of light parts, and the knight who rode a stag into your festival steps out of it on foot — bark and bronze, a helm of living silver branches, a glaive whose head is a single black thorn. You know him. He plants the glaive. *"I am Maelorn, Thorn Ward of this gate. My lord has marked you, and the marked are tested before they are met. I will not aim to kill —"* the thorn lowers until it points at the boldest of you *"— so do not insult me by dying."*

*(Player handout: **Maelorn, the Thorn Ward** — see `the-green-lords-court-3-player-handouts.md`. Full statblock, tactics, the parley triggers, and the berry shortcut are in `the-green-lords-court-2-combat-tracker.md`, Encounter 1.)*

### Quillip Arrives — and the Berry Pays Off

As the trial begins, **Quillip Burr-and-Whistle** uncurls from a hawthorn branch, more rattled than she lets on. This is the **payoff slot** for the S4 token that S5 never spent.

> [!NOTE]
> **Read-aloud:** Read Aloud — Quillip
> *"You came. Good. Bad. Both."* The pixie drops to Nalith's shoulder, wings the color of the seam. *"That's the Thorn Ward, and the door's question has a blade in it — he has to, the Court is watching and he will **not** be embarrassed."* She glances at the contracting light and her banter thins. *"You still have my berry? The hawthorn one? Now would be the time, sweetling. Save your blood for what's on the other side. You'll want it."*

### Quillip's Own Crossing *(a small, optional beat — ~1 minute)*

Quillip has dropped tokens, traded songs, and turned up at festivals — but she has **never once gone through the door,** and she is more afraid of it than her chatter admits (roster: *lonely in a way she would never admit; fears being forgotten*). Give her a beat of real choice once the gate resolves: does she dart back to the safe grey treeline, or follow the party through the seam into the lord's hall? She will not decide for free — she wants a reason.

> [!NOTE]
> **Read-aloud:** Read Aloud — Quillip at the Threshold
> The pixie hovers at the very edge of the standing light, and for once she isn't joking. *"I don't go in there. Nobody who goes in there comes back the same, and I like being **me,** thank you."* Her little wings buzz, indecisive. *"...But you'll walk in there and forget to be afraid, and someone has to remember to be afraid **for** you."* She looks at the closing seam, then at Nalith. *"Tell me to come and I'll come. Don't make me decide it alone."*

> [!WARNING]
> **Flag:** DM — Quillip's choice (seeds her into Act Two and S7)
> **If any PC invites her,** Quillip crosses with the party — a frightened, loyal, *very* useful set of fey eyes inside Oberon's hall (she'll hiss the fey-law tells in Scene 3 — *eat nothing, promise nothing* — and can be the one who survives the fall into the Feywild beside them; see Scene 4's closing and *Loot & Rewards*). **If no one invites her,** she stays at the treeline, calls one last warning after them, and is **gone when they're pulled through** — a thread left in the material world. Either way, her loneliness made visible costs the table nothing and pays off her arc. Let the players' choice stand.

> [!WARNING]
> **Flag:** DM — Quillip's hawthorn berry (the S4 token, paid off here at last)
> If Nalith **presents the berry** (shows it to the Thorn Ward or presses it to the arch), the gate-challenge collapses into pure ceremony — Maelorn lowers his glaive, the "fight" is one symbolic round of crossed blades, and the party is received as **invited guests** rather than trespassers being tested. **The berry vouches for the whole party at the arch — Dirk included** (*"vouched by the wood's own"*). If Nalith lost, ate, or planted the berry — no problem; run the full trial. Either way it ends in a yield, never a kill. See the combat tracker for both branches.

### The Unmarked Sixth *(Dirk's beat — run it before or during the trial)*

The mark is on four (Fiorn, Yinu, Naan, Nalith); **Dirk was on a barge when it landed,** and fey law does not round up. Maelorn will not pretend otherwise.

> [!NOTE]
> **Read-aloud:** Read Aloud — The Thorn Ward and Dirk
> The antlered helm turns to Dirk and the knight inhales once, like a man reading weather. *"Four my lord has marked. This one carries no mark, no invitation, and no debt — river-blood, walked into a leaving wood for people met a day ago."* A pause that might be approval. *"The marked are tested because they are expected. **You** are tested because you are interesting. Stand with them and be measured — or stand aside, and the gate has no quarrel with you."*

**Let silverstrike1 make the call.** Standing with the party means Dirk is measured alongside everyone and **counted through the door** with the rest. Standing aside is safe and Maelorn genuinely holds no grudge — but Dirk then has **no safe-conduct across the seam** and watches from the treeline with Sela's old limit. (The berry covers Dirk too, if Nalith spends it.) No kid will pick to stay behind — but the *choice* is what makes crossing mean something.

### What the Trial Measures

The fight is a **question, not a slaughter.** Maelorn pulls killing blows (a downed PC drops to 1 HP and prone — "the flat of the thorn"), and he **kneels and names the party worthy when bloodied or at the end of Round 3.** What the party shows him — courage, restraint, cleverness, mercy — he carries through the door ahead of them, and it **colors how Oberon receives them** in Act Two (track it; see file 4).

**Even the berry shortcut produces a verdict.** If Nalith spends the berry and the trial collapses to one ceremonial round, Maelorn still *reads* the party in that single crossed-blades salute — the gate exists to measure, and the measure is the thing he carries to his lord. Pick the verdict from how they've carried themselves all session (gracious / clever / wary / swaggering); it still sets Oberon's opening warmth. The berry buys past the *fight,* not past the *measuring.*

> [!NOTE]
> **DM — track the verdict (it's spoken aloud in Scene 3).** Note the one word or phrase Maelorn would use for this party: *unbroken and unbloodied,* *merciful,* *clever,* *brave,* or — if they killed him — *poachers.* He delivers it to Oberon's face when they cross (see Scene 3 setup). This is the gate's payoff made tangible at the table instead of hand-waved off-screen.

> [!WARNING]
> **Flag:** DM — Do NOT kill the Thorn Ward, and don't let him kill a PC
> If the party murders the gatekeeper, the audience curdles before it starts — Oberon receives them as *poachers,* not guests, and the harvest in Act Two loses its velvet (he need not be gracious to meat). *Ceremonial Restraint* catches any PC at 1 HP; narrate the thorn turning flat. The trial has a yield ramp every round — take it the moment they've shown him something. **Maelorn then escorts them through the seam himself** — walking *in* with them rather than reporting from a distance — and the door does not let Sela's old limit stop her now; she crosses with them. (His spoken verdict to Oberon lands in Scene 3.)

### What This Scene Locks In

- The party is **received** — tested (or vouched via the berry) and judged worthy; Maelorn's read of them precedes them through the door.
- **Dirk's standing is settled** — counted through, or holding at the treeline by choice.
- **The party crosses the seam** into the court — and **Sela crosses with them,** past the limit she has lived inside for twelve years.

# PART TWO — The Green Lord's Table

*~60 minutes. The welcome, the lord, the harvest, and the cut.*

---

## Scene 3 — The Court Received

### Setup

Through the seam, the dying grey is gone. The party stands inside the **bright center** — a hall woven from living hawthorn grown into vaulting arches, every surface hung with white blossom and green-white-bone ribbon, warm gold light pouring from nowhere. Music. The smell of every good thing. And on a throne of antler, root, and iron-grey thornwood, before the seam now shrunk to a shining vertical thread behind him, **the lord of the wood is waiting** — and he rises, smiling, to meet them like family.

> [!NOTE]
> **Read-aloud:** Read Aloud
> You step through the standing water of the door and the dying world falls away behind you. Here it is **spring without end** — a hall of living hawthorn vaulted overhead like a cathedral grown rather than built, white with blossom, warm with a light that has no source. Long tables stand laid with a feast that steams. Pipes and strings play something just past the edge of a song you almost know. And from a throne of antler and root and grey thorn, before a thread of green-gold light pulled thin behind him, a tall fey lord rises — beautiful and ancient, crowned in antler and blossom, eyes like sunlight through leaves — and spreads his arms as though you are the guests he has waited an age to seat. *"There you are,"* he says, warm as a hearth. *"Come in, come in. You are most welcome at my table. I have wanted to thank you for a long while."*

*(Player handout: **Oberon, the Green Lord** — see `the-green-lords-court-3-player-handouts.md`.)*

### Maelorn's Verdict *(deliver it the moment they cross — the gate's payoff, spoken to the lord's face)*

Maelorn walked in with them; before Oberon says a word more, the Thorn Ward plants his glaive and **reports the party's measure aloud** — the read he took at the gate, now made tangible at the table. Pick the line from the verdict you tracked in Scene 2. This is *why* the gate mattered: Oberon's warmth in the next beat visibly follows from it.

> [!NOTE]
> **Read-aloud:** Read Aloud — The Thorn Ward Reports
> The antlered knight crosses behind you and kneels once at the foot of the throne. *"My lord. I measured them at your gate."* And then — pick one:
> - **Worthy / merciful:** *"They did not break, and they did not kill. One of them stayed my own blade with a word. They are worth your table."* (Oberon's welcome runs *warmest* — he leans in, delighted.)
> - **Clever / wary:** *"They were quick, and they were careful, and they trusted nothing — not even the door. Cunning guests, lord."* (Oberon is *amused and respectful* — a host enjoying a worthy game.)
> - **Berry-vouched:** *"They came bearing your hawthorn's own berry, lord. Vouched by the wood itself. I did not need to test them long."* (Oberon is *pleased and indulgent* — *"Of course they did."*)
> - **Poachers (killed Maelorn — a different fey says it, since Maelorn is dead):** a sprite, or Oberon himself, notes it flatly: *"They spilled my Ward's blood at my own gate."* (Oberon's grace goes *thin and cold* — still courteous, but you can feel the velvet come off.)

### The Gracious Lord *(play him warm — the menace is that he is never not warm)*

This is **Oberon, the Green Lord** — an archfey of the wild wood, one of the old and powerful ones, lord of beasts and the green. He **names himself** at his own table (the reserved S5+ reveal, paid here). Play him as the perfect host: gracious, funny, flattering, generous. He thanks the party sincerely for sealing the drow cleft beneath Old Skull — *"You kept the spiders out of my wood; do you imagine I did not notice?"* He seats them, toasts them, draws each one out by name and gift.

Give a beat or two to the table — proof he has been **watching** through twelve years of Sela's ribbons:

Each thread is **a temptation aimed at a thing the player already wants** — a live S4–S5 hook turned into an offer. Don't relitigate the hook; just let Oberon dangle it, then move on. (The point is that *saying yes would actually solve something* for that PC — that's the hook in the velvet.)

| PC | His thread — *the offer behind the compliment* |
|---|---|
| **Fiorn** | *"I dropped a fey-primrose on your table at the Greening, bard, to hear what you'd make of it. You named it true and played me a falling song."* Then, lower: *"Your touring elf would have you carry his bags down a muddy road for a season. I offer you an audience that never tires, never leaves, and never forgets a single verse. Sing here, and be sung *of* forever."* (Trumps Helbrin's road-tour offer.) |
| **Yinu** | *"You read my wood better than the wizard who sleeps in your Silverhand farm."* A pause. *"You were given a little cipher-book and told it was 'a key, not a recruitment.' Sweet of them. **I** read every letter that crosses my wood — every cipher, every dead-drop, every secret the Harpers think is theirs. Stay, and read it all *from the inside.*"* (Trumps the Harper cipher / Orvyn's 'key.') |
| **Naan** | *"A little daughter of the dawn, at my green table. Your Morninglord's people in Highmoon wrote you a letter — did you know I read every letter that passes my wood?"* He smiles. *"They ask you to choose one light and walk to it. Stay, and you need never choose between two lights again — mine is the nearer, and it does not set."* (Trumps the Lathander/Highmoon invitation.) |
| **Nalith** | *"You carry one of my hawthorn's own berries and you did not spend it to harm my Ward. A guest's instinct."* Then, gently: *"You spend yourself patching a dale that buries its scouts and forgets your name by spring. Here, no one you tend ever dies. Heal a hall where healing always wins. You were always going to sit here."* (Trumps the thankless-healer thread; pairs with Sela's Scene 4 goodbye.) |
| **Dirk** | *"And the river brought me one I did not call. Unmarked, uninvited, and here anyway. The wood likes a thing it did not plan."* A nod, almost fond. *"You live on a raft on a borrowed river, sailor, one flood from nothing. I have a river that never floods and beer that does not run out. Sit. No one here asks where you came from."* (Aimed at Dirk's rootless drifter angle.) |
| **Kto** *(if present)* | *"You were not at the bonfire when my Ward marked your friends, archer — and you came anyway, after them, into a leaving wood. Loyalty is a coin I honor."* Then: *"Your long war with the spider-folk is over and it cost you a blade's worth of grief. I have hunts that never end and quarry that never runs out. Sit at my hand."* (Nods to her closed Zeldrazz vendetta.) |

Then the **offer**, laid like a gift:

> [!NOTE]
> **Read-aloud:** Read Aloud — The Offer
> *"Here is what I would give you,"* the Green Lord says, and the whole bright hall leans in to listen. *"A place. This table, this spring, this hall — forever, and no winter in it. You have spent yourselves for a grey little dale that pays you in copper and errands. I pay in **eternity.** Stay. Be mine, and be honored, and never grow old or afraid again."* He smiles, and it is the warmest thing you have seen all day. *"Say yes. It is such an easy word."*

### The Harvest Behind the Grace

The offer **is** the harvest. Everything Sela confessed — the slow pull, the taken children, the emptied wood — converges here: Oberon does not *steward* the green, he **gathers** it, reeling whole living things into his domain to be his forever, smiling and hollow. Once the seam finishes contracting, **everything inside the hall is gathered with it** — saying "yes" only makes it gracious. Seed the tell; let the players find it:

| Beat | Check | What it reveals |
|---|---|---|
| The other guests | **Insight DC 14** (Naan, Fiorn) or **Perception DC 15** | The hall is **full** of guests — fey, beasts, and *people:* farmfolk, a peddler, **children** in season-old Shadowdale clothes — all smiling, all still, all *slightly wrong,* like portraits of themselves. None of them have left this table in a very long time. (Sela goes white: *"Those are the children. The ones the wood 'took.' Gods, they're all still here."*) |
| The thread behind the throne | **Arcana DC 15** (Yinu +11) | The seam is now a single contracting thread, anchored to the throne — and **anchored to the party**, fine green-gold filaments hooked into the mark on the four. When it closes, it does not send anyone home; it **reels them in.** *Saying yes just stops you pulling against the line.* |
| The feast | **Survival/Nature DC 13**, or any fey-lore | The food is real and the warmth is real and that is the trap — **eat nothing, promise nothing.** A guest who takes the lord's bread by his own word is *gathered* by consent. (Classic fey law; Quillip will hiss it if no one rolls it.) |
| Oberon himself | **Insight DC 17** (hard) | He is not lying and never has been. He genuinely means the gift. That is what makes him terrible: to Oberon, being gathered into his eternal court **is** kindness. He will not rage if refused — he will simply let the door finish closing, because the line is already set. |
| Sela's ribbons in the court | **Investigation DC 14** or Sela's own recognition (automatic if she is present) | The ribbons woven through the hall — green, white, bone — are **Sela's knots.** She tied them. And some are not just stitching: they are **messages,** still legible in the knot-pattern, words she carried and never read. One cluster near the throne is older than the rest, the knots tighter, the ribbon sun-faded: a message she carried **years ago.** Sela goes still. *"Those are mine. I tied those. And I never… I never read what was in them."* (Sets up the drow-intelligence reveal in Scene 4.) |

**Sela's ribbons are the bridge to the drow plot.** If the party spots the tell above (or even if they don't — Sela will notice her own knots), seed the realization that the ribbons weren't *only* stitching. Some carried **words** — Oberon's instructions to fey wardens who watched the passages beneath Old Skull. Sela carried drow intelligence for years without knowing it. Do **not** reveal the content here; save it for Scene 4, when Sela reads the knots as she cuts. The beat in Scene 3 is just the *recognition* — her knots are in the court, and they say things she never read.

> [!WARNING]
> **Flag:** DM — Oberon is not a combat encounter. Do not stat him; do not let them fight him.
> The Green Lord is an **archfey in the heart of his own power.** There is no statblock in file 2 for him **on purpose.** If the party attacks, he does **not** deign to fight — he sighs, the music keeps playing, and the **seam contracts a stage faster** (advance the Harvest Clock; see file 4). Steel does nothing here. The only handle on this room is the **binding** — and the only hand that can reach it is Sela's. Steer the table off "kill him" and onto "the line, the thread, the ribbons." Quillip, Maelorn (if befriended), or Sela can all say it plainly: *"You cannot cut him. You can only cut the line."*

### The Bargain Floor *(if the party negotiates instead of refusing flat)*

Oberon **loves** a bargain — it is more binding than a feast. He will dicker, graciously, and every deal is a hook for later sessions (or a trap now). Full matrix in file 4; the shape:

- **He will let them leave** — for a price: a name, a memory, a firstborn season, a return at the next turning. Any such deal is real fey contract law; **don't let it be free, and write down what they promised.**
- **He will release the taken children** — but only in trade for someone to take their place at the table. (This is the door Sela walks through in Scene 4.)
- **He will not** give back the years he has already gathered, and he will not let the *whole* catch go for nothing. The line is set; something has to pay it out.

### What the Party CAN Change *(agency inside a fixed climax)*

The ending is fixed — **Sela cuts the line, Sela dies, the party falls into the Feywild** — and the prep rightly steers off "kill Oberon" and "out-pull the line." But a fixed *ending* is not a railroad if the players have real levers on its *terms.* Make these explicit so the table never feels like they're only watching a cutscene. Every one of these is something their choices genuinely decide:

| What they can change | How | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| **Oberon's lasting disposition** | Refuse flat / bargain / threaten / play along — *and* the gate verdict | Sets the Green Lord's stance toward them the next time the wood and the party cross (S7+). A debt owed both ways takes its shape here |
| **Whether the taken children go free** | Press Oberon for their release (a bargain), or open the gap Sela's cut creates and *hold it* long enough for them to pour through | If the party doesn't act, the cut still breaks the harvest — but the children's clean escape is **their** win, not an automatic one |
| **What they promise (or refuse to promise)** | Every bargain is real fey-contract law | What they owe Oberon follows them into the Feywild and back. Refusing a tempting deal is its own act of agency |
| **Whether Sela's last message is fully read** | Help her decode the old knot (Scene 4 — a check, or simply guarding her the extra beat) | Determines how *complete* the drow-intelligence the party carries out is (see Scene 4) |
| **Who stands with Sela, and how she's witnessed** | Stand at the throne with her, a word, a hand, Fiorn's *Falling* played one last time | Costs nothing mechanically; it's the emotional core of the night and the thing the log remembers |

> [!NOTE]
> **DM — say yes to clever non-combat plays.** If the party tries something inventive that *isn't* attacking Oberon — cutting other ribbons to slow the reel, using a spell to shield Sela's work, talking a gathered child into moving, spending Quillip's banked boon — **let it matter** within the fixed frame: it can buy a round, free another captive, or harden the party's standing. The one thing that never works is steel on the lord. Everything short of that is on the table.

### What This Scene Locks In

- **Oberon, the Green Lord, is named** — the reserved reveal, paid at his own table.
- The party understands the **welcome is the harvest:** the seam reels them in when it closes; the taken children are already here; eating or consenting completes the gather.
- The only lever in the room is the **binding** Sela tied — setting up her sacrifice. **But the *terms* of that sacrifice are the party's to shape** (the children, the promises, the message, the witness).

## Scene 4 — The Last Ribbon

### Setup

The seam is a hand's-breadth from closing. Whatever the party has tried — refusal, bargain, a doomed swing at a god — the line is set and reeling, and they can feel the hooks in the mark drawing them toward the throne. And **Sela Wyndmere** steps forward, past the party, to the foot of the throne she served blind for twelve years, with the last spool of ribbon in her hands.

> [!NOTE]
> **Read-aloud:** Read Aloud — Sela at the Throne
> Sela walks to the foot of the throne the way she walked into the wood — quiet, certain, watching the patient. She looks up at the Green Lord, and for the first time all day she is not afraid. *"You gave me healing,"* she says, *"and I gave you twelve years of knots, and never once read what I was tying. I read it now. Every ribbon I left was a stitch, and I sewed this wood into your hand a thread at a time. The children. The droughts. Them —"* she does not look at the still, smiling guests *"— I did that. Carrying your words."* Her hands move to the ribbons woven through the throne's roots, and she stops. Her fingers find a knot she tied **years** ago — and her face changes. *"These weren't all stitches,"* she whispers. *"Some of these were **messages.** Real words. I was carrying — gods, I was carrying **intelligence** and I never —"* She lifts the last knot of green, white, and bone. *"So I'll carry one more. My own. And I'll read this one to them first, because they'll need it where they're going."*

### Reading the Knot Together *(refinement — let a PC help her decode it)*

The message is in Sela's ribbon-cipher, but the hall is unravelling and her hands are failing as the healing pours out of her. **Let a PC help her finish reading it** — it makes the party *participants* in the discovery rather than an audience to a monologue, and it ties Scene 3's ribbon-recognition tell straight into this payoff.

- **The simplest version:** anyone who steadies her hands or holds the ribbon taut lets her read it in full — no roll, just the choice to help.
- **The rollable version:** **Yinu** (Arcana, to parse the binding-script) or **Fiorn/anyone** (Investigation, to read the knot-pattern) at **DC 13** surfaces the *whole* message clearly. On a help/success, Sela reads every line below. **On a failure or if no one helps,** she gets only the *fragment* — she gasps out *"the masked ones... the spider-ones... a third cleft they never found—"* and the rest is lost as the seam takes her. The party still carries the gist, but the precise wording (and the "river-stone / do not let them past" detail) is theirs only if a PC helped her read it.

> [!NOTE]
> **Read-aloud:** Read Aloud — The Last Message
> As Sela's fingers work the old knot open — a PC's hands steadying hers — the ribbon-language unravels into words only she can read, and she speaks them aloud, fast, because the hall is coming apart around her and she is running out of time. *"This one's four years old. I carried it to the hollow hawthorn and never knew what it said."* She reads:
>
> *"The dark ones dig beneath the skull-stone. The masked ones open mouths in the rock; the spider-ones close them. Both are ours to watch, not ours to fight. Hold the green at the river-stone and do not let them past the third cleft. If the stitching holds, the wood holds."*
>
> She looks at the party, and the understanding hits her like the healing leaving her body. *"He wasn't just gathering. He was **watching** them. The drow. Under Old Skull. His wardens were holding them back, and I was the — I was the **thread** that held the line, and I never knew."* The knot dissolves in her fingers. *"The stitching's broken now. I broke it. And when this cut lands, there's nothing holding that third cleft at all."*

> [!WARNING]
> **Flag:** DM — The drow-intelligence tie (connects S6 to the S1–S3 drow arc)
> This is the bridge between the fey arc and the drow arc. Sela's twelve years of unread messages weren't only the harvest-stitching — some were **Oberon's instructions to fey wardens** who monitored the drow passages beneath Old Skull. The "masked ones" are the **Vhaeraunians** (silver half-masks, S1–S3); the "spider-ones" are the **Lolthites** (S2). The **"third cleft"** is a deeper passage the party has never found — below the three staging sites they already cleared. The implication: Oberon's fey were a containment perimeter, and Sela's ribbons were part of it. By breaking the pact (S5) and cutting the binding (S6), the party has **removed that containment.** When they return from the Feywild, the drow situation beneath Old Skull will be worse than they left it. **The party carries this intelligence into the Feywild** — it's the one piece of Sela's legacy that isn't spent by her death.

### Sela Cuts the Line *(the climax — give it room)*

Sela is the **instrument of the harvest** — and therefore the only one who can unmake it. The fey healing Oberon loaned her twelve years ago is a **thread of the wood's own life woven into her;** the pact made her a living knot in his binding. She can cut the line — by **spending that thread,** giving back every borrowed year at once. It will sever the seam from the inside and break Oberon's hold on the party. It will also **kill her.** She knows. She does it anyway.

Run it as **her** moment, not a check the party can roll for her — but give the table agency *around* it:

- **The party cannot save her and stop the harvest both.** Make that clear if they try (Insight, or Quillip, or Maelorn): *"The line needs a life to pay it out. She is offering hers. You can spend it, or you can all be spent. There is no third knot."* If a PC tries to take her place, she refuses — *"You have years left to grow old badly. I spent mine. Let me spend them well."* (If a player **insists** on a heroic substitution, that's a table call — but the intended, prepped, and far stronger beat is **Sela's** death. Steer hard toward letting her have it.)
- **What the party CAN do:** stand with her, guard her as she works (Oberon does not rage, but the hall itself resists — vines, the still guests stirring, the seam thrashing), free the taken children through the gap she opens, and *witness* her — a kind word, a hand, Fiorn's falling song played one last time. Mark who stands with her; it matters to her and to the log.
- **A goodbye, not just a death — give Sela one last line *to* someone.** Her strongest played bond is with **Nalith** (S4: Sela judged the Greening, praised her healing — *"You must be a skilled healer"* — and pinned her the gold ribbon; the same moment Quillip slipped her the berry). Aim the goodbye there if Nalith is at the throne; fall back to **Fiorn** (the *Falling* song, the primrose) or whoever stood closest. Don't let the death land on the *party-in-general* when it can land on a *face.*

> [!NOTE]
> **Read-aloud:** Read Aloud — Sela's Goodbye *(to Nalith, or whoever stands closest)*
> The healing is already leaving her — the white streak in her hair going pure silver, her hands going to light. She finds Nalith's eyes. *"I pinned a gold ribbon on you at the Greening and told you you were a skilled healer. I meant it. You're the one this wood actually needed — I was only ever the courier."* A breath. *"Don't carry messages you won't read. Promise me that much."* (If Nalith isn't there, she says it to Fiorn — *"Play me the falling song. You named it before either of us knew what it meant."* — or to whoever held her hand.)

> [!NOTE]
> **Read-aloud:** Read Aloud — The Cut
> She unties the knot. It is the smallest motion — a healer's fingers, sure and gentle — and the whole hall **screams.** Green-gold light pours out of Sela, not into her, twelve years given back in a single breath; the ribbon dissolves into motes, and every other ribbon in the hall begins to unravel with it, the whole woven court coming apart at the seams she sewed. On his throne the Green Lord does not rage. He watches the line he set fray in the hands of the one who tied it, and something almost like respect crosses his ancient face. *"Ah,"* says Oberon softly. *"The courier reads at last."* The seam behind him blows open like a held breath finally let go — and it does **not** open onto the road home.

### The Tumble *(the closing image — then cut)*

The binding collapses. Oberon's hold on the party breaks — they are not gathered. But the seam, unmade from inside, does not deposit them gently back in the grey wood; the backlash **tears the door wide and pulls them through it the wrong way,** down the drain Sela just unplugged, into the bright wrong country on the far side. **Sela is not with them.** She gave the last of herself to the cut, and the unmaking took her with it — she is gone, and the wood she died to free is already greening behind the closing tear.

> [!NOTE]
> **DM — one thing falls *with* them (an S7 cold-open anchor).** The party tumbles into the Feywild with more than "no idea where the bottom is" — give the far side **one concrete thing** to grab onto, so S7 has a hook the moment it opens. Use whichever fits the table:
> - **Quillip, if she crossed** (refinement above) — she's pulled through *with* them, the one familiar fey voice on a hostile plane. The strongest anchor: she can name what they're falling toward in S7's cold open.
> - **A strand of the lord's ribbon** — as the court unravels, a length of green-white-bone ribbon snags on a PC (the one who stood closest to Sela). It is **Sela's last knotwork,** unspent — still warm, still legible to anyone who learns the cipher, and (DM discretion) still carrying *guest-right* on the far side. A tangible relic of her, and of the Court.
> - **Both,** if Quillip came and the moment wants weight.
>
> Name the object/companion now; *do not* narrate where it (or they) land. The anchor is for the S7 opening, not for tonight.

> [!NOTE]
> **Read-aloud:** Read Aloud — Closing
> The floor of the world goes out from under you. The hall comes apart — blossom and ribbon and grey-thorn throne unravelling into light — and the last thing you see in it is Sela Wyndmere, lit from inside, lifting one hand toward you that is already coming apart into petals. *Then the door has you.* Not the road home. Not the grey wood. Something **brighter** — falling color and impossible distance and a sky the wrong number of colors — and you are falling into it, all of you together — a healer's last gift burning in your chests, *(if Quillip crossed:* a small terrified pixie shrieking and clinging to someone's collar,*) (and/or:* a single strand of green-white-bone ribbon wound tight around one wrist, the last thing Sela ever tied,*)* a wood saved at your backs, and absolutely no idea where the bottom is.
>
> *(End of session. Where they land is Session 007.)*

> [!WARNING]
> **Flag:** DM — End on the fall. Do not narrate the landing.
> The image is the tumble through into the Feywild — *brightness, wrongness, together, falling.* **Stop there.** Resist every urge to describe where they come down; that first breath of the far side is the cold open of the next session, and it's worth more unspent. If players ask "where are we?" — *"You'll find out when you land."* Cut to black.

### What This Scene Locks In

- **Sela Wyndmere is dead** — redemptively, by her own choice, undoing twelve years of complicity and breaking Oberon's hold on the party.
- **The harvest is broken** — the taken children freed (if the party opened the gap), the wood greening again, Oberon cheated of his catch.
- **The party is pulled through into the Feywild** — alive, free, together, and **lost** — carrying **one concrete anchor** into S7 (Quillip if she crossed, and/or the snagged strand of Sela's ribbon). The emergence is the S7 cold open.
- **Quillip's choice is settled** — she crossed with them (invited) or was left at the treeline (not). Either outcome is a live S7 thread.
- **Oberon survives, un-defeated and un-fought** — his court unmade but his person untouched. A debt is now owed in both directions. (Long-term thread.)
- **Drow intelligence carried into the Feywild** — Sela's last message reveals Oberon's fey wardens were monitoring (and containing) the drow beneath Old Skull; the **"third cleft"** is a deeper passage the party has never found; and the fey containment is now broken along with the harvest. The party carries this knowledge with them — Sela's final gift alongside the cut.
