## Adventure Summary

Session 006 ended mid-air: Sela Wyndmere cut Oberon's ribbon and the seam threw the party out with no say in where they would land. They land on the **Salt Way**, a dead shoreline road in western Impiltur, on the floor of a sea that left decades ago — **no questgiver, no contract, no road home**, Shadowdale unreachable behind them and its drow clock running without them.

Inside the first mile they are caught in the **lighthouse snare** ⚗️: the Sarshel Light's old Impilturan light-ward — built to burn demons back off a shoreline — **reversed by the Fraternity of Tharos**. It no longer wards; it *lures*: fleeing along the road bends you back to the same **broken milestone**, and the sky overhead never moves. **Two things run on one anchor** — the **snare** (the ward reversed at the *lamp*) and **the Drawing** ⚗️ (the rite at the *top of the tower*: three fire-scribed pedestals, four bound innocents, and the seal's lesser **emissary**, drawn up through the lens to crack the **Sarshel Seal** in the seabed rock). **Tonight is the final window; the last phase is scheduled for true dawn, about three hours after the party lands.**

**Orin Vale** has kept ten travellers alive in the wreck of the *Amber Gull* — nine lured, and himself — for six weeks, and buried two. **Four of his people are bound to brackets in the lantern room**, taken three nights ago. **Tamsin Reed**, the keeper, has served inside the lamp's thrall for **three months**: not a cultist but the first victim, and the best witness to how the lamp can be repaired.

**Three wins, three different costs.** **Smash the lens** — fast: conduit broken, emissary dragged back into the seal, snare dead for everyone in the mile, **and the old ward dies with it**, leaving the seal **unwarded**. **Restore the alignment** — three arms worked by hand mid-fight: conduit resealed, emissary **banished**, **refuge preserved**, drained captives wake (subject to the three-round post-breach recovery limit). **Shutter the lamp** — one action with the keeper's key and code: the snare ends for everyone and **the Drawing runs on**. Then dawn, and nine miles east to **New Sarshel**.

> [!NOTE]
> **DM — what this is not.** No return to Shadowdale, no contract. **Sela's fate stays unresolved**, with Yinu's compromised cipher book, Naan's undisclosed Lathander letter, Oberon's disposition, and the Vhaeraunian clock (*Threads Left Open*). **The cult did not cause the portal, summon the party, or know they were falling** — say it plainly and let the table check it.

## The Night's Shape — three phases, one deadline

**Geography.** The snare is a **1-mile-radius shell centred on the lamp**. The party lands at its **west edge**, on the road by the broken milestone — **one mile west of the tower**; the camp sits **midway** (~½ mile from each) 400 ft off the road seaward, the turning beam in plain view; the tower is the centre of the two-mile-wide region; **New Sarshel is nine miles east of the tower, outside the shell.**

| Phase | What it is | Clock |
|---|---|---|
| **1 — The Lure** (Act I) | The mile holds the road: loop, visions, the camp of the caught. | **No combat clock**; the night deadline runs |
| **2 — The Approach** (2.1) | The dry seabed, the tower, the keeper in the road. Optional, non-lethal, skippable. | No combat clock; deadline runs |
| **3 — The Breach** (2.2) | Lantern room: interrupt Veyra or reach the scheduled rite at dawn | Six-segment clock begins at dawn or when confronted; see authoritative Clock start below |

**Deadline:** true dawn is three hours after landing. The fixed sky is an optical illusion; time continues. Orin’s hourglass and the dated ritual schedule reveal the deadline without a check; near dawn the false sky flickers. Use travel and player choices, not table discussion time, to track elapsed hours. The cult has spent three months corrupting the ward; three nights ago it gathered its final captives. Tonight the prepared charge will break the seal. Confrontation can force Veyra to spend that charge early; her panic creates an opportunity to interfere.

| Pace | Reaching the lantern room | What the DM runs |
|---|---|---|
| **Arrive with over one hour until dawn** | Preparations unfinished | Third pedestal unlit (counts as inactive); emissary arrives round 2. Veyra is surprised only if the normal stealth/surprise rules justify it |
| **One short rest** | Costs one hour, not an automatic encounter penalty | Use actual time remaining: early if still over one hour; otherwise normal setup. Resting does not independently alter the encounter |
| **Within the final hour before dawn** | All preparations ready | Normal setup. Veyra starts the clock if confronted; otherwise waits until dawn |
| **Long rest (8 hrs)** | After dawn | The Drawing **completes while they sleep** — play **Too Late**; announce it before the rest starts |

> [!TIP]
> **A clock, not a whip.** Skipping the camp's rest is never the wrong call — it buys a **stated advantage** at the tower. Only the world's clock prices a longer rest; never retro-fit a penalty onto a rest already taken.

# The Snare — Rules and Exits

⚗️ The reversed light-ward covers a **one-mile-radius sphere centred on the lamp**. Only the road loops; stepping off it escapes the loop, **not** the visions or false sky. Cross the physical sphere to end every local effect. A direct route from its centre to the rim takes about 20 minutes at normal walking speed, longer in rough ground. From the west-edge landing, a short off-road walk west is enough. The camp is still inside the sphere. No invisible barrier prevents leaving.

- **Loop:** travelling on the road away from the lamp folds back to the broken milestone; an attempted departure demonstrates this within five minutes. Toward the tower never loops. Off-road travel follows ordinary geography. Visions persist inside the radius but never compel movement.
- **Visions (a lure, never a leash).** Hourly on the road a creature sees its own **longing** as real. **It never dictates a PC's behaviour:** ask what the character wants most, show it, let them take **one step**, hand control back. **WIS DC 13** (or *detect magic*, or comparing notes) = **disbelieving**.
- **Rest:** time and sleep work normally. The camp offers shelter, food, and company; a long rest is possible elsewhere too, but eight hours misses the announced dawn deadline. The nail suppresses local illusions within 10 ft; it is not the only place sleep is possible.

| Exit | How | Caveat |
|---|---|---|
| **A. Step off the road** | Avoid the looping route; navigate normally toward the actual boundary | Visions remain until the boundary, without forcing behavior |
| **B. Suppress it (magic)** | ⚗️ *Dispel magic*: treat the personal glamour as a 3rd-level magical effect, ended automatically on one creature; the anchor can reapply it on entering its beam again. *Remove curse*: ends the personal mark until next dawn. *Detect magic*: senses nearby magic under its normal range/visibility rules; it does not map the sphere. *Daylight*: illumination, not an automatic dispel | Homebrew effects stated explicitly; spells keep their normal casting time, targeting, and concentration |
| **C. Cross the boundary** | Walk off-road, or use available flight/teleportation with its normal limits to cross the sphere | *Fly* is a 3rd-level spell potentially available at Level 6; check actual sheets. No invented 200-ft height or 300-ft jump escape threshold |
| **D. Follow the light** | Camp about 15 minutes from landing; tower about 30 minutes total on foot | Camp can be approached or bypassed; choices remain open |
| **E. Put the light out** | Shutter it (key + code, 1 action) or **smash the lens** (2.2) — the snare ends for everyone in the mile | Shuttering leaves the Drawing running; smashing costs the ward |

> [!TIP]
> **Hand the rules over fast.** Once anyone sees *the road is the lie*, say so plainly — players who don't know a trap's rules can't out-think it.

## Act I, Scene 1.1 — The Broken Milestone

**Purpose / transition:** Cold-open the instant S6 cut; establish the snare, the geography, the deadline, and the fifth traveller inside 20 minutes.

#### Setting

- **Location:** the **Salt Way**, an old Impilturan shoreline road across the former seabed (canon, CG p. 0145) — cracked blue-grey silt, salt-bleached pine, oyster middens, a leaning mile-stone. The **west edge of the snare, one mile west of the tower.**
- **See:** A night that is not moving — fixed moon, stars pinned, salt-fog to 120 ft — and a road unspooling both ways. **One yellow-white light turns low to the east: a lighthouse with no water to warn.** At their feet, the **broken milestone**, coral-crusted, its numeral chipped to a stub — and the chip looks fresh.
- **Hear:** No surf; the sea is miles behind. Wind scraping salt, old hulls creaking on dry ground, and **a bell-buoy tolling where no water has been for decades** — plus the same gull crying the same three notes each time the road comes back around.
- **Smell:** Dry brine, salt-dust, baked oyster shell, cold ash. **Nothing green, nothing rotting** — the air is preserved, which is the tell.
- **Sense:** *(gated)* **Detect magic** — one vast shell of abjuration under an illusion school; old, Impilturan, running **backwards**. **Divine Sense** — *desecrated consecration*: a holy ward inverted. **Passive Perception 14+** — the chip is newer each return. **Arcana DC 13** — a sea-king light-ward keyed to a seabed seal. **Insight DC 12** — the calm is manufactured; these people are *held*.

**NPCs:** None. **Encounter:** None.

##### The landing

The PCs at this table come down hard — **no damage, no lost gear, no Sela.** Play the difference: dark, flat, silent; no wood, no court, no dale. Anyone checking the stone, the flats, or the road signs can place themselves (History or Survival DC 12, or the carved letters **SAR—EL**): **Impiltur, the coast road west of New Sarshel, on ground that was underwater.**

> [!NOTE]
> **Attendance:** Nalith, Naan, Kto, and Yinu arrive from the portal. Juniper’s character witnesses them from the road and joins immediately; name, build, background, and resources follow the player’s sheet. Tyvlex was present in Session 006 but is off-camera this session; her location remains a DM/player decision. Do not invent a death, capture, or separation crisis for absent PCs.

##### Juniper's character — the fifth traveller *(any name, class, or build)*

Juniper’s character is **already on the road and witnesses the four returning PCs land**. Offer a traveling reason only if the player wants one; leave background, resources, and prior knowledge to the player. Comparing incompatible visions is their first shared discovery. No exhaustion, damage, or resource refresh is imposed. The five-player party is together from the opening minute.

##### The wander, the loop, the vision

Let them compare their visions immediately; each player chooses what their character sees and how to respond. An initial moment of disorientation is description, not forced action. The camp gives an answering lantern signal through the fog; Orin calls that four people were taken to the tower. If the party tests the road away from the lamp, the milestone returns within five minutes. Off-road movement avoids that loop; visions persist until the true boundary. No skill roll is needed to share these clues.

**Conditional follow-through:**

- **Toward the light:** camp in ~15 minutes, tower ~30 minutes from landing. Scene 1.2 is an offered stop, not mandatory; if bypassed, the names of the missing reach them in Orin’s warning and the restoration clues remain at the tower.
- **Off-road:** ordinary movement; the camp’s answering lantern remains an option. Walking west over the actual boundary ends local effects. Do not redirect that choice to the camp.
- **Out of the mile (C):** let it stand. Play **Walk Away** — road east open, the camp's people still caught, the four bound when the window opens. State the cost once, then play it. **Never body-block them back onto the road.**
- **Otherwise:** the loop returns them to the milestone, the visions sharpen, the cook-fire in the *Amber Gull*'s ribs shows seaward. → **Scene 1.2.**

## Act I, Scene 1.2 — The Stranded Camp

**Purpose / transition:** A place, a face, and a valve — and the whole plot (timeline, tally, keeper, the missing four) handed over **without a mandatory skill check**, with the missing known **before** the party chooses to go up.

#### Setting

- **Location:** the ribcage of the ***Amber Gull***, a Telflamm trader left high and dry when the sea went out sixty years ago — **400 ft off the Salt Way, midway between landing and tower**: hull for wall and roof, tarps to the masts, a cook-fire of scrap iron, **a row of salt-stained boots nailed along the rail — one pair per traveller still living.**
- **See:** Hides against the wind; a tally of the missing cut into the rail; a bent **iron nail** on a leather cord by the fire, the salt-fog visibly *not* touching it — the one dry, quiet patch in the mile. **Ten living people:** Orin at the fire with his tally-book and a boot on his knee, **eight** asleep or staring, **Ilde Marsh** sitting apart facing the light. From the fire the beam sweeps low over the flat.
- **Hear:** Low voices that stop when the party arrives; someone counting under their breath without noticing; the fire's hiss, hull copper flexing, that bell-buoy.
- **Smell:** Woodsmoke, old tar, wet wool, weak tea, salt crust — and under it, faint but real, **rendered tallow and burnt hair**, which the camp has stopped noticing and the party should not.
- **Sense:** *(gated)* **Detect magic** — the nail carries one live thread of the *old* light-ward: within 10 ft the glamour goes quiet. **Divine Sense** — the ground is desecrated in a long patient line running at the tower. **Arcana/Religion DC 13** (or a plain look at the ledger page) — demon-naming fire-script, the Fraternity's induction rite writ small. **Insight DC 12** — not addled; *hopeful*, which is worse.

**NPCs:** Orin Vale; Ilde Marsh; the eight other lured; and — by name — the four the tower took.

##### Orin Vale — the man who kept them alive ⚗️

**Visual — show the handout when they first see them, and read these cues aloud:**
- Wiry, weather-beaten, grey stubble — and unhurried: he finishes a boot-stitch before he looks up.
- A rope-belt with a **bent iron nail** hanging off it, on a leather cord.
- Keeps the camp fed and counted, and keeps his own tally-book close.
- Wants the light **out**, does not care how, and will show you what he has written down.

- **Disposition going in:** wary for ten seconds, then instantly useful. Six weeks at this camp; **two people buried**; strangers who walked in *without* walking to the light are the first good news he has had.
- **Personality (play him warm):** practical, unhurried, task-first. He mends boots while he talks and hands out tea in a fixed order, **counting the cups**, a habit he never explains and never stops. He says little of himself, remembers everything about everyone else, is **ashamed** of the two who died before he understood the road, wants his four back and the light **out** any way, and is quietly **afraid** of what is wearing his friend's coat.
- **History:** a supply courier who knows the old coast road; Tamsin is his friend. Six weeks ago she gave him a ward-nail, a dated warning, and a copied maintenance page during a lucid interval. Her original log stays in the tower. Since the four were taken three nights ago, he has tried twice to reach them; Tamsin drove him back at the door. Andry escaped briefly from the tower during a transfer, brought out the torn ledger schedule, then was recaptured while guiding the other prisoners. Orin keeps that fragment with his tally.
- **Count:** fifteen other travelers arrived over six weeks: nine now at camp (Ilde plus eight), four captive upstairs, two died of exposure during early attempts to leave. Orin makes ten living people at camp. They know off-road travel is possible but most stayed hoping for missing kin; Orin stayed to rescue his friends. Offer evacuation plainly: they are vulnerable and reluctant, not magically unable to step outside.
- **Evidence, no roll:** Tamsin’s dated warning identifies the reversal and asks Orin to protect travelers. The copied maintenance page shows the three alignment arms, watch-words, and shutter mechanism. His tally accounts for the fifteen travelers. Andry’s ledger fragment names the four captives and tonight’s dawn deadline; it does not reveal every New Sarshel contact. Only Veyra’s complete ledger can supply those addresses.
- **Possible outcomes and effects:** **shelter offered** (the warded berth — see *Rest & Recovery*). **Alienated:** he prioritizes evacuation rather than assisting at the tower; residents still make their own choices.

##### Ilde Marsh — the one still walking toward it ⚗️

- **Disposition:** friendly, unbothered, unarguable. **Capable, calm, immovable** — the camp's **organizer** (water, food, fire, boots, the whole order of the place) and the reason it has not fallen apart. Likes Orin; thinks he has given up.
- **Motivations:** her drowned son stands in the lamp-room door, dry and grown. Turned back **nine times — never by the loop**, which has never caught her walking *that* way, but by **the keeper driving her off the tideline** and by **the vision fading** as the lamp turned and the doorway came up empty.
- **Choices:** Ilde can accept conflicting-vision evidence without a mandatory check, organize evacuation, or follow the party seeking closure. If she approaches Veyra, allow warnings and interception. Seizing her requires a successful ordinary grapple; binding her requires another action on a later turn while still grappled. Neither action advances the clock. She is never teleported upstairs or automatically added as a penalty.

##### The eight others ⚗️

Scared, courteous, patient in a way that reads as hopelessness. **The differing visions:** ask three, get three unrelated things (a wife lost in the Tasseldale, a boat never wrecked, a purse of gold, a roofed house, a daughter's wedding) — **nobody sees the same thing and everybody sees the same light.** Hand it over free: it makes the party smarter than the snare and turns Ilde. Anyone freed goes quiet a while, grieving the vision.

##### The four the tower took — Orin's people ⚗️

**Name them at this table, with their faces** — they were campers here for weeks, so the party meets their stories before the brackets and the rescue is a rescue, not a tally.

- **Halen Croy** — a bargeman's boy, twelve. Counts things to steady himself: the boots on the rail, the gull's calls, the days. Orin taught him knots; he was quick and proud of it.
- **Merra Voss** — a pedlar who worked the Salt Way for years before the road closed. Knows every traveller's name in camp; kept its accounts on a slate until the night she walked at the light.
- **Bett Ralen** — a fisher's widow, strong in the arms, short with fools. Mends nets, boots, and tempers; talks to the absent sea like a neighbour.
- **Andry Sull** — a caravan guard. Fought when the tower's woman took him and limps because of it; knows a little cult fire-script from a caravan job that went wrong, and knows he knows it.

**Encounter:** None.

#### The night's clock at the camp

- **Short rest — safe:** normal benefits in the warded berth; sleep still does not come deep.
- **The ⚗️ DM dial** *(once per session, DM's call)*: if the table is out of gas, the berth clears **one exhaustion level** per PC and returns **one expended slot of 3rd level or lower, or one limited class resource** — **apply it even if they skipped the camp.** A valve, not a payment.
- **Long rest — too late:** sleep comes here and **the Drawing finishes while they sleep.** Announce the cost, then play **Too Late.** A legitimate ending.

**Conditional follow-through:**

- **Brief rest, then move (intended pace):** the final phase is under way as they arrive — **Encounter 2 as written.**
- **Leave at once, no rest:** they arrive **ahead of schedule** and interrupt the preparations (pace table above).
- **Camp survives with Orin protected:** the *Restore* ending is materially better — his people keep a warded refuge instead of fleeing a hostile flat.
- **Otherwise:** they know there is a keeper, a captive count, a cult operator with a ledger, and a lamp that can be put out. → **Scene 2.1.**

## Act II, Scene 2.1 — The Dry Seabed

**Purpose / transition:** Cross the flats, put the watchkeeper in the road, and make the optional fight solvable with a document, a phrase, or a walk-past.

#### Setting

- **Location:** the **Drywater** — former seabed, cracked silt walled back from the road by a fossil tideline of barnacled rock and dead coral, wrecks at anchor on dry ground. At the road's bend, the **Sarshel Light**: octagonal, ~90 ft, lower third barnacled, old waterline scored around it, lamp turning yellow-white.
- **See:** The fossil tideline, a wall of shell and coral a man high; oyster beds like paving; a three-masted wreck bolt upright, sails still furled. The door: iron-banded oak, a carved **Impilturan ward-sigil** cut *backwards*, packed with fresh ash. **On the apron of bare rock, the keepers' maintenance markings** — a long cut line and **three sigil-marks**, the last cut with the sea-kings' sign — with the tower's shadow lying across them **not moving the way the fixed sky says it should.** High in the lamp-room, a silhouette that does not move.
- **Hear:** A deep brass groan every half-minute, audible from 200 ft; salt crunching like glass; wind in the gallery. Standing still: **a very large, slow thud** through the rock about every ten seconds — something pressing up from underneath.
- **Smell:** Salt, low tide without a tide, cold iron, lamp-oil — and over it **hot metal and burnt resin.** A fire fed on purpose.
- **Sense:** *(gated)* **Detect magic** — a sea-kings' light-ward reversed at the root, *recently* and *deliberately*, on the same anchor as the seal below. **Arcana/Religion DC 13** — cut from a book, not a tradition: broken grammar, unmistakable intent. **Investigation DC 13 (or free from the log extract)** — the three alignment-marks on the gimbal ring match the three on the apron: **the lens can be turned back by hand.** **Passive Perception 15+** — the silhouette has not moved. **Insight DC 13** — Tamsin is defending a *person*, not a building; she thinks they are wreckers.

**NPCs:** Keeper Tamsin Reed (and, unseen above, Veyra Sorn).

> [!NOTE]
> **The restoration is explained before the fight.** By the stair the party can know the lens has **three alignment arms** — from the **apron markings** (free look, or Investigation DC 13), **Tamsin's log extract** (her own alignment procedure), or **Tamsin herself** once calmed. Hand it over: the lantern room is a fight about physical work, not a secret.

##### Tamsin Reed — keeper of the Sarshel Light, enthralled ⚗️

**Visual — show the handout when they first see them, and read these cues aloud:**
- Fifty-something, sun-cracked, in a coat black with salt.
- A brass hand-lantern and a gnarled staff; other keeper’s tools hang by the doorway.
- She warns you off three times before she swings, and she means all three.
- She says you are **wreckers**. She says the light is hers. She will not let you up her stair.

- **Disposition:** protective and suspicious, charmed for three months but eating and sleeping normally. Calls strangers wreckers and warns them three times. The captives’ names and her own maintenance notes create visible doubt before any roll.
- **Personality:** patient, dryly funny beneath the charm; checks lantern wicks mid-conversation, insists everyone use the stair handrail, and corrects sloppy nautical terms. Duty and affection for Orin conflict with the implanted order to guard the door. Once herself, she is horrified by the deception and immediately starts helping.
- **Motivations:** keep the light burning, keep the stair clear. She believes it is the last thing between the Drywater and something worse — **and she is right**, which is the tragedy of the scene. Not a cultist; never recruited.
- **What she knows:** **Veyra Sorn came to her door as a ward-repairer** ⚗️ — a commission and a customs writ, sent "to service the old light-ward before the season turns." Tamsin believed her, let her in, opened the lamp for her, and has spent three months watching what that cost. Lucid intervals let her write, think, and **drive Orin back off the tideline** rather than let him die on her stair.
- **Possible outcomes and effects:** **talked down** — Orin's extract read aloud, the **watch-words** (free, no roll), ***remove curse*** (⚗️ same ruling as the snare), ***dispel magic* DC 15**, or **Persuasion/Insight DC 13** with the captives' names; she then hands over **lantern-key and shutter-code** and asks to come up as a witness, not a fighter. **Subdued** — non-lethal to 0 HP leaves her **unconscious and stable**, key, code, and logbook on her belt (takeable). **Walked past** — **her pursuit is bounded and stated, not magical:** the charm binds her to the tower base and she will not cross the fossil tideline; she shouts, does not follow, and makes no further attacks once they are inside. **Killed** — the light gutters, the alarm carries: **Encounter 2 starts at segment 1**, no other scaling, and the **shutter route remains available** ; recover the key and code from her belt.

##### Veyra Sorn — present, unseen, working above ⚗️

Unaware until the alarm or the lamp-room; three nights at the pedestals. If the alarm reaches her (Tamsin killed, alarm raised) she gains **+1 segment** and the emissary is ready. If the party comes up quietly — Tamsin calmed, blinded, or bypassed, plus stealth or *silence* on the stair — she is **surprised** and Encounter 2 opens a round early for them. Full entry in 2.2.

**Encounter:** Encounter 1 — *Keeper Tamsin Reed and the Turning Light* (File 2; its part 6 carries the authoritative written layout and the map).

**Conditional follow-through:**

- **Tamsin calmed or subdued:** 2.2 opens with **key and code**, alarm unfired, emissary late (lighter scaling, File 2).
- **Tamsin killed:** **2.2 starts at segment 1**, no other scaling.
- **Party never enters the tower:** play **Walk Away** — cost stated plainly, then the dawn road.
- **Otherwise:** climb the stair. → **Scene 2.2.**

## Act II, Scene 2.2 — The Lantern Chamber

**Purpose / transition:** The main event: a ritual with a clock, four people bound to it, one operator, one demon, and a lens the party can break, fix, or shutter. **All three answers are wins; each costs something different.**

#### Setting

- **Location:** the lantern room at the top of the tower — a **60-ft circular chamber** under an open brass dome and the snare's fixed sky, reached by a **stone stair rising from the south.** Layout and map: File 2, Encounter 2, part 6.
- **See:** **The lens at the centre** — a 10-ft brass-and-crystal mechanism on a gimbal ring, taller than a man, full of light that has nowhere to go, cradled in **three alignment arms**; **three fire-scribed pedestals** (N, E, W), each on a chalked circle of demon-names whose wax lines burn down toward the window; **four iron brackets around the lens cradle**, four people bound to them; **Veyra Sorn** between the pedestals with a fire-iron and a ledger; the light around the lens **shaped like a body** — a brute outline of brine and grit half in, half out of the glass; and on the south wall the keeper's log-hooks, the shutter lever, and the keeper's line and hoist-hooks.
- **Hear:** The lens's brass groan, continuous, pitched to a whine; Veyra's recitation, a flat patient list of names that never rises; the captives' fast shallow breathing; **and a huge, slow, wet knock from directly beneath the floor**, ten seconds apart, answering her.
- **Smell:** Lamp-oil burnt to resin, hot brass, ozone, and a **living brine smell** — deep water and blood — where there has been no water for eighty years. Under it, tallow and burnt hair.
- **Sense:** *(gated)* **Detect magic** — pedestals, lens, and seal are one circuit **facing the wrong way**; the reversal reads as a direction, not a secret. **Arcana/Religion DC 13** — the induction rite writ large: innocents named and burned to feed a breach. **Investigation DC 13** — the alignment arms carry marks matching three on the gimbal ring. **Insight DC 14** — Veyra is not stalling and not afraid; she thinks she has time, which means she is wrong about something. **Passive Perception 15+** — the pedestals feed the light, and the light feeds the floor.

**NPCs:** Veyra Sorn; the Brine Fiend; the Bound Four.

##### Veyra Sorn — cult operator of the Fraternity of Tharos ⚗️

**Visual — show the handout when they first see them, and read these cues aloud:**
- Dark braided hair, charcoal coat with brass fittings, black gloves and an astrolabe.
- Gloved hands adjust a brass instrument; a ledger and fire-iron rest on the nearby bench.
- She does not shout and she does not gloat — she **recites**, a flat, patient list of names.
- Around her, **three rings of names are burning in the floor.**

- **Disposition going in:** unsurprised by armed strangers, and honest that she is not the reason they are here — **she will say the one true thing they need: the Fraternity did not bring them here.** Let the table check it, let it stand.
- **Personality:** calm, courteous, talkative while she works; she recites rather than shouts and never gloats — she *explains*, because that is how the Fraternity recruits and because she is a scholar who thinks she is the most interesting person in the room.
- **Motivations — recognition, and the fear of going back empty-handed:** a **Fraternity scholar** whose standing rests on being the one who opened the Sarshel Seal — the name in the ledger's margin, the operative the Demonstone remembers. The reversal cost her months; the cell gets **one window a night**; the Fraternity does not forgive a wasted season. **That fear is the lever:** offer her any way to walk away **not empty-handed** — her life, the ledger, a story that blames the weather, a promise nobody follows her — and she genuinely considers it, and keeps it exactly as long as it profits her.
- **Possible outcomes and effects — bargains, state-dependent; write the offer to fit the board, never against it.** *Nothing freed yet:* she offers the captives, cutting two brackets herself as proof and keeping two as insurance — the party walks out with two living people and the promise of the rest, and the lens and ledger stay hers. *Captives already freed:* she cannot un-free anyone, so she offers the reverse — walk out with everyone, no pursuit, and she names **what is under the seal** and where the Fraternity's next beacons are, because a live failure who kept the lens is worth more to her than a dead success. **She never asks for a freed captive back, and does not need captives after she releases them.**
- **Fights, flees, dies:** **fights** in Encounter 2, using the pedestals as cover, the clock as a hostage, the emissary as a bodyguard. **Flees** below 25 HP with the emissary gone — onto the **gallery**, and **no 90-ft drop:** the south wall carries the **keeper's gear** (line and hoist-hooks for cleaning the lamp) and she takes the line over the rail and down the tower's west face, unhurried and competent, into the salt-fog. She escapes **wounded and named** and the Fraternity learns the party's faces — **let her run when the dice allow it.** **Killed:** the ritual does not stop on its own; her **+1** goes away and nothing else changes, and her ledger is in the party's hands.

##### The Brine Fiend — the seal's emissary ⚗️

**Visual — show the handout when they first see them, and read these cues aloud:**
- The room was dry, and then it was not.
- A shape made of **standing brine** — gristle, grit, and salt packed into an outline about as tall as a person and twice as wide.
- A **hooked mouth** it can open much further than the rest of it.
- **Where it stands, the stone weeps.**

- **Disposition going in:** half-manifested, ravenous, no interest in the party as people. It wants the lens, the salt, and the four warm things on the brackets.
- **What it is — and is not. Keep this clean at the table:** a **lesser demon and the emissary of the thing sealed**, a shape sustained by the intact, misaligned lens (the pedestals power the ritual, not its body). **It is not the Sarshel Seal's prisoner.** The prisoner is the **thing under the floor** — far larger, never manifested tonight, that slow ten-second knock. **Do not stat it, do not name it, and do not let the table think the fight in this room is with it.**
- **Possible outcomes and effects:** **killed** — it breaks into **brine-slurry and one hooked glass-hard tooth** (see *Loot*) and its shape is drawn back into the seal; **the ritual is entirely unaffected and the clock keeps ticking** — **a fight won, not the night won**, and not permanently destroyed. **Banished** if the alignment is restored: pulled back through the resealed conduit, no corpse, no tooth. **Returned with the light** if the lens is smashed: dragged back the same way, same residue, same tooth, and the ward above it dies. **Escaped** if the clock reaches 6 with the lens intact: it walks out into the Drywater and the ending changes.

##### The Bound Four — the camp's missing ⚗️

- **Disposition going in:** terrified, gagged, bound to iron brackets round the lens cradle. Orin can name every one; the party met their stories at the camp.
- **What the ritual does — no automatic deaths:** at **segment 2** and again at **segment 4** it **drains** a bound captive — the rite takes warmth and breath for the pedestals and they go **unconscious and stable, not dead and not dying.** **The drain is reversible: if the alignment is restored, or the lens is resolved in time, drained captives wake.** At **segment 6** the seal cracks and the two still bound are in immediate, visible, reachable danger — **a rescue window with a survivable breach, never an unavoidable kill** (numbers in File 2, *Triggers*).
- **Helpful, safe tasks on rescue** *(not combatants; no stat blocks; off the board, helping only inside the party's own actions — never with invented powers):* **Halen Croy** knows the stair in the dark and counts the steps aloud, leading the freed down and out; **Merra Voss** reads the maintenance marks the way she read road signs and confirms aloud which way an arm turns; **Bett Ralen** is a second pair of hands — a body on the gimbal is a body on the gimbal; **Andry Sull** knows enough cult fire-script from a caravan job to name what burns in the floor, and to say it is answerable.
- **Possible outcomes and effects:** **one action at each visible safety catch, no roll**, to free them (or use bracket damage/dispel; File 2). **−1 segment per captive, once each.** **A freed captive cannot be killed remotely or re-taken by the ritual** — off the bracket is out of the conduit. **All currently bound captives freed, or no pedestal burning, stops the Drawing; after segment 6 the breach must also be repaired** — say it out loud; that is the reward for choosing people over damage.

**Encounter:** Encounter 2 — *The Lantern Chamber* (File 2).

##### The Lens — the objective *(an object, not a creature)*

- **Smash it:** an ordinary object, **AC 15, HP 30, damage threshold 10** — a single blow under 10 does nothing, and there is **no guaranteed three blows**; small weapons chip, spells must legally target objects. **Result:** the snare ends instantly for everyone in the mile, the emissary is dragged back into the seal, and **the old ward dies with the light** — refuge unprotected, seal left **unwarded.** Fast, cheap, permanent.
- **Restore the alignment:** **three arms, any order, any PC, no one-per-round limit.** Each arm is completed **either** by **one action plus a successful DC 13 check** (Arcana, Religion, Investigation, or Sleight of Hand — whatever the character is doing) **or** by **two actions with no roll at all**, working the arm across two turns; **progress persists between rounds.** **No accelerated spells** (*mending* keeps its 1-minute cast and is not a combat action), **no invented class powers, and no single skill gates the room** — a failed check costs a turn, never the run. **Result:** the demon threat is **banished**, the **refuge is preserved**, everyone still alive stays alive, and **drained captives wake.** A minimum of three successful actions, or six no-roll actions, distinguishes restoration from destruction; failed checks and interference can cost more.
- **Shutter it:** with **Tamsin's key and shutter-code**, the lamp is snuffed in **one action** at the south wall. **Result: the snare ends for everyone in the mile — and the Drawing does not.** The pedestals burn on their own circuit and the clock runs full speed, **no pause, no grace period.** The lever is **manual and stays where it is put**; working it back and forth buys nothing — say so the first time anyone tries.

##### The Drawing — the ritual clock *(one deterministic rule; identical in File 2)*

- **Resolution limits:** threshold events occur once each; a freed named target cannot be drained. After segment 6, restoration must finish within three further rounds to reseal; later repair ends the snare but leaves a regional breach. Smashing before 6 leaves an intact unwarded seal; after 6 leaves a cracked unwarded seal. Drained captives wake on restoration/destruction of the lens, ordinary healing, or an hour of care outside the snare. These limits apply to every ending below.

- **Clock start:** Veyra starts the six-segment breach at true dawn (three hours after landing), or rushes it early when confronted in the lantern room. An alarm readies her but does not itself start the clock. If unopposed after dawn, she advances twice per round and completes it in three rounds. During a played confrontation use normal initiative; elapsed time still matters if the party retreats. Start at 0; an announced alarm adds 1 once, never stacks. If the rite has already completed, use the breach aftermath, not a fresh clock.
- **Advance:** +1 at the end of each round while at least one captive is bound AND at least one pedestal burns. Veyra may spend her action for +1 under the same conditions. Maximum +2 per round. If either condition fails the clock stops; Veyra cannot restart it by herself.
- **Veyra's action:** if she works the rite, **+1 more, once per round, maximum. Cap: +2 in any round. The emissary can never advance it.**
- **Setbacks, each counted once and never farmed:** freeing a captive **−1** (per captive, once — a freed captive is out of the conduit and cannot be re-bound to be freed again); destroying a pedestal **−1** (per pedestal, once). **Minimum 0.**
- **Stop:** no captives remain bound OR no pedestal burns. The counter freezes; no creature can reactivate destroyed pedestals or rebind a rescued captive during this encounter. Stopping after 6 does not repair the breach; use the three-round recovery window.
- **At 6:** no automatic death. The party has three further rounds to repair the lens and reseal the breach; smashing it halts the conduit but leaves a cracked seal. After that, the regional breach persists even if the lens is resolved; the stair stays open for rescue or retreat. Apply only the written floor hazard, not a surprise explosion.

##### Ending the session

| Outcome | How | Cost | Carried out |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The Light Turned Back** | All three arms completed | At least 3 successful actions or 6 no-roll actions, plus interference; *Counter-Ward* can undo an arm per round | Demon **banished**; refuge **preserved**; drained captives **wake**; seal sealed; Fraternity named, not ended |
| **The Glass Broken** | Lens to 0 HP | The old ward dies: refuge gone, seal **unwarded** — deferred, not solved | Every lured traveller freed at once; emissary **returned**; slurry and tooth as evidence; an intact but unwarded seal nine miles from a city |
| **The Lamp Shuttered** | Key + code, one action | Snare ends; **the ritual does not**, and does not pause | A dark lantern room, pedestals still burning, and a race — smash, restore, or clear out |
| **The Drawing Completed** | Clock 6 with the lens intact, or a long rest through the window | Seal cracks; demonic pressure bleeds into the Drywater | The *Too Late* flight east — survivors, evidence, a live incursion behind them |
| **The Walk Away** | Party crosses the boundary without resolving the tower | Veyra can finish at dawn unless someone intervenes; camp residents can evacuate if persuaded or warned | Freedom and whatever people/evidence the party actually brought; no forced return or surprise punishment |

**Aftermath:** ending the snare reveals the actual sky; it does not advance time. If the party wins early, dawn still lies ahead. Rescued survivors can rest, evacuate, or keep the restored waystation. Merra offers introductions to honest traders in New Sarshel; evidence can earn a hearing rather than predetermined rejection. New Sarshel is nine miles east of the tower (about three hours at normal pace, longer with injured people). Veyra’s contacts are available only if the party obtained her ledger or questioned her. The Demonstone tower is published canon (CG p. 145), but its identity is not automatically player knowledge. Close on departure or shelter according to player choices.

**Conditional follow-through:**

- **Restored:** local ward and seal repaired; surviving NPC relationships and recovered evidence become future opportunities. Orin can keep the waystation. City addresses are a lead only if actually acquired.
- **Smashed:** the **seal is unwarded**; the refuge must move and the wreck is abandoned. Carry forward: **an open seal** under a road nine miles from a city of thirty thousand, the **tooth** as evidence, and **what became of Tamsin Reed.**
- **Shuttered only:** the **Drawing runs on** — a short clock in a dark room, the seal the live stake.
- **Completed:** the **seal cracks** and demonic pressure bleeds into the Drywater. Carry forward: **an active incursion nine miles from New Sarshel**, Orin's survivors walking east.
- **Veyra escaped:** she can report what she actually saw, subject to her survival and choices. She does not automatically know who holds the ledger. If captured, interrogation may reveal leads. Killing her does not transfer equipment remotely; recovery still requires searching the room.
- **Campaign-wide:** **no Shadowdale, no Sela, no fey resolution** — the drow threat under Old Skull stays live, Oberon's disposition unknown, and Yinu's cipher book, Naan's letter, and the Vhaeraunian clock all tick.

# Rest & Recovery in the Snare ⚗️

**No automatic rest.** The party comes off the Feywild with whatever they had when Sela cut the ribbon; the landing costs them nothing, but nothing in the mile hands them sleep either.

| Situation | What they get |
|---|---|
| **Open ground** | Normal rest rules; exposure and the announced dawn deadline matter, not magical insomnia |
| **Camp** | Food, shelter, first aid, and a normal short rest. No automatic recovery of spell slots or class resources beyond the characters’ actual abilities |
| **The camp, long rest (8 hrs)** | Sleep comes — and **the Drawing finishes while they sleep.** Announce first, then play *Too Late* |
| **After the lens is resolved** | Ground is normal: **a long rest is available**, as the falling action |

> [!TIP]
> **Weariness is texture, not a punishment.** Nothing retro-punishes a rest the table already took, and nothing spirals: if they arrive genuinely out of gas, hand them the dial and run the fight they came for.
